In a real model the ambient space has thousands of dimensions and no single axis is asked to carry everything: features are stored as directions in superposition, so one neuron can participate in many concepts at once.

Why this matters

Embeddings are the model's only entry point for meaning, and the geometry is load-bearing. Similar directions mean substitutable contexts, which is why cosine similarity retrieves sensible neighbours and why analogy arithmetic sometimes works. It also explains the failure modes: directions are reused, so "close" is not the same as "correct", and a vector can drift toward an unrelated cluster when a word is polysemous. In a high-dimensional space most vectors are nearly orthogonal, which is what lets a single layer store many features without them interfering — until they do, and the model confuses two things that share a direction.

Concept: word embeddings, cosine similarity, superposition.
