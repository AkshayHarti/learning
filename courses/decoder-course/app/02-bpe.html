Tokens are learned, not given

Byte-pair encoding builds a vocabulary bottom-up by merging whichever adjacent pair is most frequent, over and over, until the budget runs out.

Why this matters

Tokenization is the layer where text stops being text, and most "model can't do X" complaints start here. Arithmetic breaks because digits arrive in arbitrary chunks; uncommon scripts break because they were rare in the training corpus and got few merges; repetition is cheap because frequent strings become single tokens. Every token also costs money and context: a tokenizer that needs 30% more tokens for your language makes every call 30% more expensive. None of this is learned by the transformer — it is frozen before training begins, and the model has to work with whatever segmentation it inherits.
